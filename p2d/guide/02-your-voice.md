# Lesson 2 · Dial in your voice

*No writing samples, no prompt engineering — a few plain questions, answered once.*

## What you'll do

Set a voice profile so p2d writes the way *you* write — and prove it to yourself by generating the same home in two different voices.

## Why this exists

Template AI copy has a tell: it all sounds like the same enthusiastic stranger. Your clients hired *you*, and your copy has carried your tone for years. p2d doesn't ask you to upload old listings or fiddle with prompts — it asks you a handful of questions a human editor would ask, and turns the answers into standing instructions for the writer.

## The questions

**The one that matters most — overall tone:**

| Option | The feel |
|---|---|
| **Literary** (default) | Editorial, master-stylist prose — vivid and evocative, never purple |
| **Architectural estate** | Trophy-tier — architecture, provenance, scale, rarity |
| **Evocative** | Lush, atmospheric, sensory |
| **Polished** | Warm, refined, professional |
| **Understated** | Confident and quiet; lets the home speak |

**The fine-tuning, if you want it** (each has a sensible default):

- **What should the opening lead with?** — architecture & design, the experience of the space, setting & views, or the single standout feature
- **Sentence rhythm** — long and flowing, or short and assured
- **Emphasis** — specifics or atmosphere
- **How long** — target length for the marketing piece
- **How should it end** — the style of the close
- **A free note** — anything a good editor should know: *"never use 'boasts'"*, *"no exclamation points"*, *"avoid 'oasis'"*

Some taste is built in regardless: clichés, exclamation marks, and superlative pile-ups are edited against by default. Your profile steers *within* good writing, not around it.

## Try this: one home, two voices

1. Open a listing you've already generated.
2. Set tone to **Architectural estate**, opening to *architecture & design* → generate.
3. Switch to **Understated**, opening to *the experience of the space* → generate again.

Read the two openings side by side. Same facts, same photos, same guardrails — two recognizably different agents. Neither one invented a thing: voice changes the *telling*, never the *truth*. The grounding check runs identically on both.

## When to change voice

- **Per listing.** A glass-and-steel architectural and a gated family compound may deserve different registers. The profile is yours to set per generation.
- **Match the seller.** Read your seller's own language in your first meeting; pick the tone they'd recognize as their home.
- **Keep a house style.** Or answer once, never touch it again, and every listing ships in your signature register.
