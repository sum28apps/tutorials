Lesson 4 · Five formats, five channels

One checked draft, every channel covered — and the weakened claim can't sneak back in.

What you'll do

Know what each format is for, where to paste it, and why all five stay consistent with each other.

The five

1. Marketing description

The long-form piece — your property site, print brochure, personal website. This is the source draft: everything else derives from it after grounding, softening, and compliance have run. Which means a claim that was softened in the marketing copy is softened everywhere — nothing weakened can leak back into a shorter format.

2. MLS remarks

Public remarks, ready for your board: fit to length limits and contact-information stripped (most MLSs prohibit agent contact info in public remarks — p2d removes it so the field passes review the first time). Paste and go.

3. Headline

Ten-or-so words for ad creative, flyers, signage, and portal titles. The single most striking, supported idea of the home.

4. Social caption

Sized for Instagram and Facebook — tight, sensory, restrained with hashtags. Pairs with your best two or three photos; post-ready.

5. Email

Subject line and body for your buyer list and fellow agents. The subject earns the open; the body hands over the essentials and the link.

All five come from Write the copy in the same run, and Save as PDF captures the complete set — clean enough to hand a seller as the "here's the campaign" page.

A sane 20-minute publishing order

  1. MLS first. The listing goes live with clean, compliant remarks.
  2. Marketing description to your site and the brochure file.
  3. Email to your list while the listing is fresh.
  4. Social caption with your three best shots.
  5. Headline into whatever ads you're running.

Every channel, same facts, same voice, zero rewriting between them.

If a format needs adjusting

  • Tone or emphasis off in one format? A targeted AI refine ("tighten the social caption") re-checks the result — see Lesson 5 for what refines cost (your first on each listing is free).
  • A quick word swap? Manual edits are always free. Note that hand-edited text is your own — the Trust Report vouches for what p2d wrote and checked.
